Where I was wrong

I built a confident thesis, then ran three things at it: a steelman told to destroy it, a replication audit, and a check against the field's consensus documents.

Four of my citations died.

Almost nothing that's an effect-size claim. So the three points of view rest on what instruments are blind to:

  • A percentage can't tell a guess from knowledge — a third of your correct answers.
  • "Evidence-based" is a claim nobody here can support, us included.
  • Retention optimisation was never a readiness claim. There is no exam in that objective function.
4:45 – 5:30 Beat of silence after the third point. FSRS minimises study-minutes-per-recall-probability — the schedulers are honest; the inference downstream isn't.

What that forced us to build

  • The engine refuses to emit a score below an evidence threshold — a refusal, not a number with wide error bars.
  • On iOS a score is an enum, so rendering a refusal as a number is a compile error.
  • Coverage runs against the official AAMC outline, all 31 categories — so what you haven't studied is visible.
  • Readiness reports on the real 472–528 scale with its range and reasons, or is withheld.

The guarantee is structural, not a promise in a README.

5:30 – 6:15 Each POV had to change code or it wasn't a POV. If they want a demo: Tools → Transfer Report shows 1 of 31 topics covered and refuses to project, naming why. 614 tests passing. 20/20 unclean kills, zero corruption.

What would prove me wrong

If confident-and-wrong rate predicts outcomes no better than raw accuracy, the thesis is dead.

Two objections I haven't answered, in the document rather than buried:

  • The memory-minus-performance gap is a difference score — reliability around .50.
  • Our intervals are too narrow: they model sampling error when the dominant variance is passage-to-passage.

A tool whose pitch is honest measurement doesn't get to hide its own weakest numbers.
